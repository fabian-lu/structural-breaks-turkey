## Structural Break Analysis: Turkey Consumer Confidence (June 2008)

**What Changed:**
The Turkish consumer confidence index experienced a downward structural shift of 2.12 points in June 2008, dropping from an average of 101.92 (above-average optimism) to 97.53 (below-average pessimism). This represents a transition from positive to negative consumer sentiment relative to the long-term average.

**Key Observations:**
- The break reversed a pre-existing declining trend, with confidence stabilizing at the lower level and beginning to recover
- Volatility decreased slightly (standard deviation fell from 2.21 to 2.03)
- The 67% confidence level suggests moderate statistical significance

**Likely Causes:**
The timing strongly suggests this break was triggered by the **global financial crisis**, which intensified dramatically in mid-2008:

- **Bear Stearns collapse** (March 2008) and subsequent banking sector stress
- **Oil price spike** reaching record highs around $140/barrel in June-July 2008
- **Emerging market contagion** as global investors fled riskier assets
- Turkey's vulnerability as an emerging economy with current account deficits and external financing needs

**Significance Assessment:**
This represents a meaningful but not catastrophic shift in consumer sentiment. The relatively quick stabilization and recovery trend suggest Turkish consumers, while affected by global uncertainty, maintained some underlying resilience. The break captures the moment when global financial stress began meaningfully impacting Turkish household confidence, preceding the more severe impacts that would follow with Lehman Brothers' collapse in September 2008.
