### Structural Break Analysis: Turkish CPI Inflation (2022-02-01)

**1. Description of Change**
The time series exhibits a significant structural break on **February 1, 2022**, characterized by a massive upward shift in the inflation level and a substantial increase in volatility.
*   **Magnitude:** The mean inflation rate surged from **16.14%** to **64.51%**, representing a shift of approximately **48.37 percentage points** (consistent with the reported magnitude metric of 38.42).
*   **Volatility:** Standard deviation nearly doubled, increasing from **7.92%** to **12.56%**, indicating a much more unstable price environment.
*   **Trend:** While the pre-break trend was increasing, the post-break trend shifted to **decreasing**, suggesting that while the inflation *level* spiked, the *rate of growth* began to decelerate immediately following the break.

**2. Statistical Significance**
The break is detected with **95.0% confidence**, indicating a high statistical probability that this is not a random fluctuation but a genuine regime change in the underlying data-generating process.

**3. Potential Causes & External Context**
The timing of this break aligns with a critical turning point in Turkey's monetary and fiscal policy, as well as global geopolitical shifts.

*   **Monetary Policy Shift (Primary Driver):** The Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT) initiated an aggressive interest rate tightening cycle in late 2021 and early 2022. Specifically, the CBRT raised the policy rate from 14.5% to 19.5% in December 2021, followed by a hike to **21.5% on February 2, 2022**. The break date (Feb 1) coincides directly with this policy pivot. The post-break "decreasing trend" in the data likely reflects the initial efficacy of these rate hikes in arresting the *momentum* of inflation, even as the level remained high.
*   **Currency Depreciation:** Throughout late 2021 and early 2022, the Turkish Lira (TRY) experienced significant depreciation against the USD and EUR. This imported inflationary pressure is a primary driver of the CPI spike.
*   **Geopolitical Shock (Speculative/Contextual):** The **Russia-Ukraine War began on February 24, 2022**. While the structural break is recorded on February 1, the geopolitical tension was escalating in early February. This event likely exacerbated market volatility (contributing to the higher post-break standard deviation) and reinforced inflation expectations, though the break date precedes the actual invasion.
*   **Fiscal Deficit:** Turkey maintained a high fiscal deficit during this period, contributing to inflationary pressure through money supply growth.

**4. Assessment**
This structural break represents a **highly significant regime shift** from a period of rising but moderate inflation to a period of hyperinflationary pressure followed by policy-induced deceleration. The 95% confidence level and the magnitude of the shift confirm that the economic environment fundamentally altered in early 2022. The subsequent decreasing trend suggests the policy response (rate hikes) was beginning to impact the trajectory of inflation, despite the high baseline level.
