### Structural Break Analysis: OECD Consumer Confidence Index (Turkey)
**Break Date:** June 1, 2008

#### 1. Nature of the Change
The structural break on June 1, 2008, represents a statistically significant downward shift in consumer sentiment.
*   **Magnitude & Direction:** The index shifted from a pre-break mean of **101.92** (indicating above-average optimism) to a post-break mean of **97.53** (indicating below-average pessimism). This represents a drop of approximately **4.4 points** in the amplitude-adjusted index.
*   **Volatility:** Standard deviation decreased slightly from 2.21 to 2.03, suggesting that while sentiment dropped, the immediate volatility narrowed post-break.
*   **Trend Reversal:** The trend shifted from a pre-break *decreasing* trajectory to a post-break *increasing* trajectory. This suggests the break marked a trough, with consumer confidence beginning to stabilize or recover slightly immediately following the shock.

#### 2. Potential Causes and External Events
The timing of this break aligns with a convergence of domestic monetary tightening and the intensification of the Global Financial Crisis.

*   **Domestic Monetary Policy (Primary Driver):**
    *   **Event:** The Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT) aggressively raised interest rates to combat high inflation. In **May 2008**, the policy rate was raised to 15.5%, and it was increased further to **16.5% in June 2008**.
    *   **Impact:** Higher borrowing costs and expectations of sticky inflation typically dampen consumer spending and sentiment, explaining the shift from optimism to pessimism.

*   **Global Financial Context:**
    *   **Event:** The US Subprime Mortgage Crisis was accelerating in mid-2008. While the Lehman Brothers collapse occurred in September 2008, credit market stress and capital outflows from emerging markets began intensifying in **June 2008**.
    *   **Impact:** As a high-growth emerging market, Turkey is sensitive to global liquidity shocks. The onset of global risk aversion likely contributed to the drop in confidence.

*   **Inflationary Pressure:**
    *   **Event:** Turkey's annual inflation rate was approximately **11.3% in June 2008**, one of the highest in the region.
    *   **Impact:** High inflation erodes purchasing power, directly correlating with the drop in the consumer confidence index.

#### 3. Significance Assessment
*   **Statistical Significance:** The magnitude of the break (2.12) indicates a significant structural change rather than random noise. The shift crosses the psychological threshold from above-average (100) to below-average sentiment.
*   **Economic Impact:** A shift of this magnitude typically signals a contraction in near-term consumer spending, which can dampen GDP growth and increase unemployment pressure.

#### 4. Analyst Note
*   **Speculation Flag:** While the June 2008 interest rate hike is a confirmed policy event, linking the *exact* break date (June 1) to the rate hike (announced late May/early June) is standard for time-series breaks, but the market reaction may have been cumulative over the preceding weeks.
