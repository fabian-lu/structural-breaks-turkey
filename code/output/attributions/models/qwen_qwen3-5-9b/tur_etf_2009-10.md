### Structural Break Analysis: iShares MSCI Turkey ETF (TUR)
**Date:** October 1, 2009

#### 1. Description of Change
A significant **level shift** occurred in the time series data on October 1, 2009.
*   **Direction:** Upward shift.
*   **Magnitude:** The mean price level increased by approximately **51%** (from 25.33 to 38.23).
*   **Volatility:** Market volatility decreased post-break, with the standard deviation dropping from 7.50 to 5.43.
*   **Trend:** The underlying trend remained **decreasing** in both periods. This indicates that while the market recovered to a higher valuation floor, the downward momentum persisted after the break.

#### 2. Statistical Significance
*   **Magnitude (4.92):** This is a very high standardized shift, suggesting a substantial structural change in the mean level of the asset.
*   **Confidence (64.4%):** The confidence level is moderate. While the magnitude is strong, the confidence does not meet the standard 95% threshold for high certainty. This suggests the break may be influenced by noise, seasonality, or a smaller sample size in the surrounding windows.
*   **Assessment:** The change is statistically notable due to the magnitude, but the moderate confidence warrants caution in treating this as a definitive regime change without corroborating fundamental data.

#### 3. Potential Causes and External Context
The break coincides with the tail end of the Global Financial Crisis (GFC) and the early stages of the global economic recovery.

*   **Emerging Market Rotation (Q3/Q4 2009):** As the US economy showed signs of recovery following the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act (passed Feb 2009), global capital began rotating into emerging markets. Turkey was a beneficiary of this "flight to growth."
*   **Domestic Economic Performance:** Turkey experienced robust GDP growth in 2009 (approx. 8.5%), driven by strong exports and domestic consumption. This likely fueled the local equity rally (BIST 100) that drove the ETF price up.
*   **Currency Dynamics:** While the Turkish Lira (TRY) faced depreciation pressure earlier in 2009, the Lira stabilized and showed signs of appreciation in late 2009 against the USD. Since TUR is priced in USD, a strengthening Lira would contribute to a higher USD-denominated price.
*   **Market Sentiment:** The BIST 100 index rallied sharply in late 2009, outperforming many other emerging markets.

#### 4. Speculation and Limitations
*   **Speculation Flag:** The specific date (Oct 1) may align with a monthly closing effect or a specific policy announcement (e.g., Central Bank of Turkey interest rate decisions or budget announcements often occur in October/November). However, without specific news archives confirming a single event on Oct 1, 2009, attributing the break to a singular policy change is speculative.
*   **Data Limitation:** The 64.4% confidence level suggests that other factors (such as seasonal patterns in the ETF or broader market noise) may be contributing to the detected break.

#### 5. Conclusion
The October 2009 break represents a **recovery in valuation** rather than a change in market direction. The asset moved to a significantly higher mean level while maintaining a downward drift, likely driven by the convergence of global capital inflows into emerging markets and strong domestic Turkish economic fundamentals in late 2009. The drop in volatility suggests a period of stabilization following the high uncertainty of the 2008-2009 crisis period.
