## Structural Break: Turkish CPI Inflation, February 2022

### What changed
The series shifts from a moderate-but-rising inflation regime (pre-break mean ≈ 16.1%, s.d. 7.9) to a high-inflation regime (post-break mean ≈ 64.5%, s.d. 12.6) — a level shift of roughly +38 percentage points in the fitted model and a ~4x increase in the mean rate. Two secondary features matter:

- **Volatility rises ~60%** (7.9 → 12.6), consistent with a loss of nominal anchoring rather than a one-off price-level jump.
- **Trend reverses**: increasing before the break, decreasing after. The post-break window captures the peak (~85% in October 2022) and the subsequent mechanical decline driven by base effects rather than genuine disinflation.

The estimated date (2022-02) should be read as the point at which the shift became statistically persistent; the actual monthly acceleration began in **December 2021** (headline inflation moved from ~21% in November 2021 to ~36% in December and ~49% in January 2022).

### Likely drivers (timing-consistent, some interpretation)
1. **Monetary policy easing into a depreciating currency.** The CBRT cut the policy rate from 19% to 14% between September and December 2021 despite accelerating inflation, under the government's "new economic model." Real policy rates turned deeply negative.
2. **Lira collapse.** TRY fell from roughly 8.5/USD in mid-2021 to ~18/USD around 20 December 2021 (~44% depreciation over 2021), with very high pass-through to import-intensive Turkish consumer prices. The FX-protected deposit scheme (KKM) announced 20 December 2021 stabilised the currency but did not undo the pass-through already in train.
3. **Administered price and wage resets, 1 January 2022.** Large electricity (up to ~50–125% for some tariff brackets) and natural gas increases, plus a ~50% minimum wage rise, delivered a concentrated January shock and second-round effects.
4. **Russia's invasion of Ukraine, 24 February 2022** — directly coincident with the break date. Turkey is a large net energy importer and was heavily dependent on Black Sea grain, so the global energy/food price spike amplified and prolonged the shock.

### Significance
The break is strongly supported: a 38-point level shift against pre-break volatility of ~8 points implies a very large signal-to-noise ratio, consistent with the 95% confidence flag. Economically, this is a regime change, not a transitory spike — inflation did not return to pre-2022 levels in the subsequent years, so post-break statistics should not be pooled with pre-break data for forecasting or variance estimation.

**Caveat:** the attribution above is inference from timing and known policy events; the data alone establish the shift's size, date, and persistence, not its causes.
