## Structural Break Analysis: Turkish CPI Inflation, October 2024

### What changed

The series shifted down by roughly 18 percentage points at the break point, with the surrounding regime means falling from ~64% to ~37% year-on-year. Two features stand out:

1. **Level shift within an ongoing downtrend.** Both regimes show a decreasing trend, so this is not a turning point but a step-down in the level of an already-disinflating series. The break most likely marks the midpoint of the steepest phase of decline rather than a discrete event.
2. **Volatility compression.** The standard deviation more than halved (12.9 → 5.5), indicating the post-break disinflation was far smoother and more monotonic than the erratic 2023–24 period. This variance reduction is often as economically meaningful as the mean shift, as it points to a more predictable price-setting environment.

### Likely drivers

- **Base effects.** The dominant mechanical factor. Turkish prices surged after the May 2023 elections — sharp lira depreciation (June–July 2023), large fuel and consumption tax increases (July 2023), and administered price adjustments. As those months rolled out of the 12-month window through H2 2024, headline YoY rates fell steeply even with unchanged monthly momentum. Headline inflation peaked around 75% in May 2024 and fell to roughly 49% by September 2024 and 44% by December 2024.
- **Monetary tightening.** The CBRT raised its policy rate from 8.5% (June 2023) to 50% (March 2024) and held it there through mid-2024 before beginning cuts in December 2024. The lagged effect of that tightening, alongside macroprudential credit growth caps, compressed domestic demand.
- **Exchange rate policy.** A managed, slow nominal depreciation of the lira implied real appreciation, damping imported-goods and pass-through inflation — a key break from the 2021–23 dynamic.
- **Wage and administered price moderation.** No mid-year minimum wage adjustment in July 2024, and the January 2025 increase (~30%) was well below the prior year's (~49%) — reducing the wage-price impulse in the post-break window.

### Significance assessment

The magnitude is large and clearly economically meaningful. However, the 68.6% confidence is only moderate, which is consistent with the underlying process: because disinflation was gradual and continuous, the break-date estimate is diffuse and the exact placement (October 2024) should not be over-interpreted. Any month between roughly July 2024 and January 2025 would plausibly fit.

**Caveat:** the post-break level (~37%) remains extremely high by international standards; the break signals a shift in the *pace and stability* of inflation, not a return to price stability.
