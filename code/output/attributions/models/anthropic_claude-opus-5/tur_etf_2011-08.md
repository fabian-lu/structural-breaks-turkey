## Structural Break Analysis: TUR, August 2011

### What the statistics show

The estimated break is a **downward level shift of ‑10.45 (roughly ‑25% relative to the pre-break mean)**, i.e. after controlling for the underlying upward drift in the fitted segments, the series re-based to a materially lower level from August 2011 onward.

Note the apparent tension in the diagnostics: the raw window means rise (35.80 → 39.03) while the fitted shift is negative. This is typical when a break is estimated within a trend-plus-level model — the pre-break window contains a steep run-up and an abrupt reversal, so the *level* parameter drops even though the average of the trailing observations is higher. It also helps explain the **low confidence (40.8%)**: the break is not a clean mean shift but an inflection embedded in a trending, high-variance series. Volatility does compress after the date (σ 8.95 → 6.20), consistent with a regime that moved to a lower, more range-bound level after a violent repricing.

### Plausible drivers (timing-consistent, some speculative)

1. **Global risk-off shock, August 2011.** The US debt-ceiling standoff and S&P's downgrade of the US sovereign rating (5 Aug 2011), plus escalation of the euro-area crisis to Italy and Spain, triggered a broad EM equity drawdown. High-beta, external-financing-dependent markets like Turkey were hit hardest.
2. **Turkish lira depreciation.** USD/TRY moved from roughly 1.65 in mid-2011 toward ~1.90 by late 2011. Because TUR is USD-denominated, currency losses compound local equity declines — a key reason a Turkey-specific USD level shift can exceed the global EM move.
3. **Monetary policy divergence.** The CBRT cut its policy rate 50bp to 5.75% on 4 Aug 2011 (an easing move into a risk-off environment), while simultaneously intervening in FX markets. This unconventional mix was widely read as tolerating lira weakness, pressuring USD returns.
4. **Macro vulnerability.** Turkey's current account deficit was running near record levels (~9‑10% of GDP) in 2011, making the market a focal point for balance-of-payments concerns once global funding conditions tightened.

### Assessment

The **economic narrative around August 2011 is strong**, but the **statistical evidence for a discrete break at that exact month is weak-to-moderate (40.8%)**. The 2011 decline in TUR was a multi-month process (roughly Q2 2011 through Q4 2011) rather than a single-month regime change, so break-detection algorithms will place low confidence on any one date. Treat this as marking the onset of a lower-level, lira-impaired regime rather than a precisely dated discontinuity.
