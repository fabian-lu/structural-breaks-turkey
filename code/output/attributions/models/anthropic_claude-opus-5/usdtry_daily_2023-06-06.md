## Structural Break Analysis: USD/TRY, 6 June 2023

### What Changed

**Level shift:** The mean exchange rate jumped from 19.80 to 24.77 — a ~5.0 lira (≈25%) upward shift in the 30-day post-break window, i.e. a step depreciation of the lira rather than a continuation of the prior drift. Notably, the pre-break series was almost flat by TRY standards (std dev 0.46, ~2.3% of level), consistent with a heavily managed/defended rate.

**Volatility regime change:** Standard deviation tripled from 0.46 to 1.42 (≈5.7% of level). This is arguably the more informative signal: the break is not just a repricing but a transition from an administratively suppressed, low-variance regime to a market-determined, high-variance one. Both windows trend upward, so the depreciation trend persisted — the break is a discrete jump layered on an existing trend.

### Likely Drivers

The timing aligns tightly with Turkey's post-election policy pivot (high confidence on chronology, moderate on causal weighting):

- **28 May 2023:** Erdoğan wins the presidential runoff, removing election-related incentive to defend the currency.
- **3–4 June 2023:** Mehmet Şimşek appointed Treasury and Finance Minister, signalling a return to orthodox economic policy.
- **7 June 2023:** The lira fell roughly 7% in a single session — its sharpest one-day drop since the December 2021 crisis — as state banks reportedly stepped back from FX-selling intervention, allowing pent-up depreciation pressure to clear.
- **9 June 2023:** Hafize Gaye Erkan named Central Bank Governor; **22 June 2023:** policy rate raised from 8.5% to 15%, beginning the tightening cycle.

The mechanism is therefore best read as a *deliberate abandonment of FX suppression*: reserves had been depleted defending the ~19–20 level, and the new economic team permitted a correction toward market-clearing levels. The rise in variance reflects the withdrawal of the intervention that had been artificially compressing it.

### Significance

Statistically strong: the mean shift (~5.0) is roughly 11 pre-break standard deviations, and the break is corroborated by an independent change in the second moment. The stated 86.1% confidence is likely conservative — probably reflecting the trending, non-stationary nature of the series, which complicates level-shift detection. The change was persistent (USD/TRY did not retrace toward 20), confirming a genuine regime change rather than a transient spike.

**Caveat:** the 30-day post-break window captures the initial adjustment only; the subsequent path was shaped by ongoing rate hikes through 2024, so parameters estimated here should not be extrapolated forward.
