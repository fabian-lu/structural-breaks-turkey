## Structural Break Assessment: Turkey CCI, April 2010

### What changed

Three things shifted simultaneously, which is what makes this a genuine level break rather than noise:

1. **Level:** Mean rose from 97.43 to 101.15 (+2.33 estimated shift, ~+3.8 points in raw means). Critically, the series crossed the 100 threshold — moving from *below-average pessimism* to *above-average optimism* for the first time in the post-crisis period.
2. **Volatility:** Standard deviation collapsed from 2.31 to 0.81 (~65% reduction). The post-break regime is not just higher, but far more stable — consistent with sentiment settling after a turbulent adjustment period.
3. **Trend:** Direction reversed from decreasing to increasing, so the break is a turning point, not just a step in an ongoing drift.

The magnitude (2.33) is roughly one pre-break standard deviation but nearly three post-break standard deviations — the shift is far more decisive when measured against the new, calmer regime.

### Likely drivers

The timing aligns closely with Turkey's sharp V-shaped recovery from the 2008–09 global financial crisis:

- **Macro rebound:** Turkish GDP contracted ~4.8% in 2009, then grew ~9% in 2010, with Q1 2010 y/y growth in the double digits. Industrial production and capacity utilisation were recovering strongly through H1 2010.
- **Monetary policy:** The CBRT had cut its policy rate to a then-record low of 6.50% (November 2009) and held it there through 2010, supporting credit growth and consumer durables demand.
- **Labour market:** Unemployment peaked near 16% in early 2009 and was falling steadily through 2010 — historically the dominant driver of Turkish consumer sentiment.
- **Asset prices:** The ISE-100 was posting record highs in spring 2010, reinforcing the "recovery has arrived" narrative.

Notably, the break occurs *just before* the escalation of the euro-area sovereign debt crisis (Greece's bailout request, 23 April 2010; programme agreed 2 May 2010). That Turkish confidence rose and stabilised through this period is consistent with the contemporaneous "Turkey decoupling" narrative — domestic demand and banking-sector strength dominating external headlines. This interpretation is plausible but goes beyond what the two windows alone can establish.

### Significance and caveats

- **Confidence is moderate at 69.2%** — below conventional thresholds. Treat this as a probable rather than confirmed break.
- The comparison windows are short (a 30-day window on monthly data implies very few effective observations), so the mean and variance estimates are imprecise and the exact break month should not be over-interpreted; the true transition may span Q1–Q2 2010.
- The strongest evidence here is the **variance reduction combined with the trend reversal and threshold crossing**, rather than the level shift alone. A 2.33-point move in an amplitude-adjusted index is economically modest in isolation.

**Bottom line:** A moderate-confidence regime shift marking the transition from post-crisis pessimism to a stable, mildly optimistic consumer sentiment regime, consistent with Turkey's 2010 growth rebound, falling unemployment, and accommodative monetary policy.
