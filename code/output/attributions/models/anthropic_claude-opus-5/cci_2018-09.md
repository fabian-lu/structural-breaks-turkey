## Structural Break Analysis: Turkey Consumer Confidence, September 2018

### What changed
The series shifted down by ~2.93 points, from a mean of 101.1 (marginally above the long-run average) to 97.9 (clearly pessimistic territory). Two features stand out:

- **It is a level shift, not a trend change.** The pre-break segment was drifting lower with very low dispersion (σ = 0.51); the post-break segment is tighter still (σ = 0.39) and mildly *rising*. This is the signature of a one-off shock that reset the level and was then partially absorbed, rather than a gradual deterioration.
- **Signal-to-noise is high.** A 2.9-point move against sub-0.5-point standard deviations is a very large displacement relative to local volatility — economically the shift is unambiguous, even though the reported statistical confidence (67.4%) is moderate, likely reflecting the short estimation windows and the fact that the pre-break period was already declining (making the break date somewhat ambiguous within a two- to three-month band).

### Likely drivers
The timing coincides almost exactly with the **Turkish lira crisis of August–September 2018**, one of the sharpest confidence shocks in the series' history:

- **Lira collapse (August 2018):** TRY fell roughly 25–40% against the USD over the month, with the most violent moves around 10–13 August, following the escalation of the US–Turkey dispute over the detention of pastor Andrew Brunson and the US decision (10 August 2018) to double steel and aluminium tariffs on Turkey.
- **Inflation shock:** CPI inflation accelerated sharply through Q3, reaching ~24.5% year-on-year in September (released 3 October 2018), directly eroding household purchasing power — historically the dominant driver of Turkish consumer sentiment.
- **Aggressive monetary tightening:** The CBRT raised its policy rate by 625bp to 24% on 13 September 2018, stabilising the currency but sharply raising borrowing costs for households.
- **Policy response and stabilisation:** The "New Economic Programme" announced on 20 September 2018 and the subsequent lira recovery in Q4 are plausible explanations for the *upward* post-break trend — confidence stabilised at a lower level once the currency stopped falling.

Note that the OECD amplitude-adjusted CCI is a smoothed, standardised transformation of the national survey, so an August shock will typically register as a September break — consistent with what is observed here.

### Assessment
This is a genuine regime shift rather than noise: it moves the index across the neutral 100 threshold and persists. Its interpretation is straightforward — an exchange-rate and inflation shock, not a slow-building cyclical downturn. The main caveats are the short comparison windows (limited observations on either side for monthly data) and the modest break-date confidence, which means the precise month should be treated as approximate (August–October 2018) even though the existence and direction of the shift are well supported.
