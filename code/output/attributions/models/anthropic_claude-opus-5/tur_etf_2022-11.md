## Structural Break Analysis: TUR ETF, November 2022

### What changed
The series shifted from a flat regime averaging **$19.10** to one averaging **$33.70** — a level increase of roughly **+$11.75 to +$14.60 (≈65–76%)**. Relative to pre-break volatility (σ = 2.18), the shift is more than five standard deviations, so it is economically and statistically substantial. Two secondary changes reinforce the break:

- **Regime character**: the pre-break period was *stable/range-bound*; the post-break period is *trending upward*.
- **Volatility**: standard deviation rose from 2.18 to 3.54 (+62%), consistent with a higher price level plus a more momentum-driven market.

The moderate confidence score (72%) is consistent with the shape of the move: this was not a single discontinuous jump but the point at which a steepening, multi-month ramp accelerated, which makes the exact break date somewhat ambiguous.

### Likely drivers

**1. The BIST 100 retail-driven equity boom under deeply negative real rates (primary factor).**
The CBRT cut its policy rate repeatedly through H2 2022 — from 14% in July to 9% by 24 November 2022 — while headline CPI peaked at **85.5% in October 2022** (released 3 November 2022). With real deposit rates around −70%, Turkish savers rotated heavily into equities as an inflation hedge, and Borsa Istanbul retail account numbers surged. The BIST 100 roughly tripled in lira terms in 2022, with the sharpest leg in Q4.

**2. A managed, unusually stable lira.**
Critically for a USD-denominated ETF, USDTRY was held near 18.5–18.6 for most of H2 2022 via the FX-protected deposit scheme (KKM) and state-bank intervention. Because the FX drag that normally erodes Turkish equity returns in dollars was suppressed, the local-currency rally passed through almost fully into TUR's USD price — the key reason the break appears in this series and not merely in local indices.

**3. A depressed base.**
The ~$19 pre-break level reflected the aftermath of the December 2021 lira collapse, so part of the magnitude is mean-reversion from a low starting point rather than a pure re-rating.

### Interpretation and caveats
This is best read as a **genuine regime shift in level and trend**, driven by a policy configuration (rate cuts into hyperinflation plus FX pegging) rather than by improving fundamentals in dollar terms. It is therefore a *policy-dependent* regime. Two subsequent events tested it and should be considered when extrapolating: the **6 February 2023 earthquakes**, which triggered a market halt and multi-day exchange closure, and the **post-May 2023 election policy pivot**, when rate normalisation and lira depreciation (USDTRY moving from ~19 to ~27 in mid-2023) sharply reduced USD returns. Any forecast built on the post-break trend should be treated as conditional on the FX regime, not as a structural improvement.
