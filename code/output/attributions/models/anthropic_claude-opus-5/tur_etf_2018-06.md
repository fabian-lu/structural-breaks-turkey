## Structural Break Analysis: TUR ETF, June 2018

### What Changed

The series shifted from a mean level of ~$30.4 to ~$20.0 — a **34% downward level shift** (magnitude 11.73) — accompanied by a reversal in trend direction (from rising to falling). Notably, absolute dispersion fell slightly (σ: 3.33 → 2.39), but *relative* volatility rose (CV: 11% → 12%), i.e. the ETF became proportionally more volatile at a much lower price base. Given the comparison windows span roughly 30 monthly observations on each side, this is a **persistent regime change**, not a transient drawdown: the pre-2018 trading range was never reclaimed.

### Likely Drivers

The timing coincides precisely with the onset of the **2018 Turkish lira/balance-of-payments crisis**, which hit both legs of this USD-denominated ETF (local equity prices *and* the TRY/USD rate):

- **May 2018:** Lira slid from ~4.0 to ~4.7/USD; CBRT held an emergency meeting (23 May) raising rates 300bp to 16.5%, followed by a hike to 17.75% on 7 June — signals of loss of policy credibility rather than reassurance.
- **June 24, 2018:** Snap presidential/parliamentary elections; Erdoğan's win under the new executive-presidency framework, combined with his publicly stated view that high interest rates cause inflation, raised investor concern over central-bank independence.
- **July 9, 2018:** Appointment of Berat Albayrak as Treasury/Finance Minister, read by markets as further concentration of economic policy.
- **July–August 2018:** Escalating US–Turkey rift over the detention of pastor Andrew Brunson; US sanctions on Turkish ministers (1 Aug) and Trump's doubling of steel/aluminium tariffs (10 Aug) triggered the acute phase — the lira touched ~7.2/USD on 13 August, roughly a 40% depreciation from May.
- **Backdrop:** Large external financing needs, ~25% inflation by late 2018, and Fed tightening/EM outflows (the broader 2018 EM stress that also hit Argentina).

### Significance

The 11.7-point shift is roughly 3.5× the pre-break standard deviation, making it statistically and economically large. The 76.9% confidence likely reflects that the decline was somewhat gradual (spread over May–August 2018) rather than a single-month discontinuity, so the exact break date is uncertain within a ~2–3 month band. Economically, this marks a durable re-rating of Turkish assets in USD terms driven primarily by currency depreciation and a higher risk premium on policy credibility — the ETF has not since returned to its pre-2018 level.

*Note: the event linkages above are consistent with the timing and widely documented, but the attribution of the price shift to any single event is inferential.*
