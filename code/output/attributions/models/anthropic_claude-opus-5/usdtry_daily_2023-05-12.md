## Structural Break Analysis: USD/TRY, 12 May 2023

### What Changed

The break marks the end of a de facto administered exchange-rate regime and the start of a market-determined depreciation.

- **Level:** 30-day mean rose from 19.37 to 21.45 (+10.7%); the reported 0.16 up-shift is consistent with a log-scale jump.
- **Volatility:** the dominant change. Standard deviation increased ~15x, from 0.11 (0.6% of the level) to 1.69 (7.9% of the level). Pre-break variability was implausibly low for a floating EM currency — a statistical fingerprint of heavy official management.
- **Trend:** both windows trend upward (lira weakening), but the pre-break path was a smooth, near-linear crawl; the post-break path is a steep, discontinuous slide.

### Likely Causes (event-linked, well documented)

- **14 May 2023 – Turkish presidential/parliamentary first round; 28 May runoff won by President Erdoğan.** Ahead of the vote, state banks and the CBRT are widely reported to have sold large volumes of FX to hold the lira in a narrow band near 19.4–19.5, which explains the near-zero pre-break variance and the depletion of net FX reserves into negative territory.
- **Post-election policy pivot.** After the runoff, official defense of the rate was scaled back and the lira was allowed to converge toward market clearing levels. Key confirming appointments: **Mehmet Şimşek as Treasury/Finance Minister (3 June 2023)** and **Hafize Gaye Erkan as CBRT Governor (9 June 2023)**, followed by the first rate hike of the tightening cycle (22 June 2023, 8.5%→15%).
- **Single-day repricing on ~7 June 2023**, when the lira fell roughly 7% (~21 → ~23), sits inside the post-break window and accounts for most of the variance increase.

### Assessment of Significance

- **Economically:** highly significant — a regime change, not a fluctuation. The shift is permanent in direction and in variance; USD/TRY did not return to the 19–20 band.
- **Statistically:** the 53.8% confidence is modest, and appropriately so. The break is primarily a **variance break** with the largest level moves arriving ~3–4 weeks after the flagged date, so the detected date should be read as the onset of the pre-election/post-election transition rather than a precise level discontinuity. A break test on volatility, or a date around 29 May–7 June, would likely yield higher confidence.

### Caveat

The attribution to FX-intervention withdrawal is inference from the volatility profile plus publicly reported policy events; the data alone establish the timing and magnitude of the regime shift, not the intervention mechanics.
