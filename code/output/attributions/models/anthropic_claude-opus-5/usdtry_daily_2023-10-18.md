## Structural Break Analysis: USD/TRY, 18 October 2023

### What Changed

The mean level of USD/TRY shifted up from **27.27 to 28.44** (+1.17 lira, or **+4.3%**) between the 30-day pre- and post-break windows. Two features are notable:

- **Direction is unchanged:** both windows exhibit an increasing trend. This is not a reversal or a new depreciation episode, but rather a **level shift along a persistent upward drift**.
- **Volatility fell slightly** (σ: 0.37 → 0.29). A rising level combined with *falling* absolute dispersion means realised volatility dropped materially in relative terms (~1.36% → ~1.02% of level) — the signature of a tightly managed, low-variance crawl rather than a disorderly move.

### Statistical Assessment

Confidence of **58.6%** is weak, and the reported magnitude (0.25) is small in standardised terms. Because both windows trend upward at a similar pace, a mechanical mean difference of this size is largely what one would expect from the underlying drift alone. **This is better characterised as a possible mild acceleration in the depreciation crawl than as a genuine regime change.** Break-detection algorithms routinely flag such points in trending series, and the low confidence score is consistent with that interpretation.

### Plausible Drivers (contextual, mid-October 2023)

- **Post-election policy normalisation:** Following the appointment of Mehmet Şimşek (Treasury/Finance, June 2023) and Hafize Gaye Erkan (CBRT, June 2023), the central bank moved from suppressing the exchange rate to allowing a controlled, steady lira slide alongside aggressive tightening (policy rate 30% on 21 Sep 2023 → 35% on 26 Oct 2023). Gradual relaxation of FX-protected deposits (KKM) and administrative controls over H2 2023 permitted more market-driven, but still smoothed, depreciation.
- **Inflation differential:** Turkish CPI was running near 61% y/y in October 2023, mechanically requiring continuous nominal depreciation; roughly 4% per month is close to purchasing-power-parity–consistent drift.
- **Global risk backdrop (speculative as a direct cause):** The Israel–Hamas conflict beginning **7 October 2023** raised regional risk premia, energy prices and dollar demand; US 10-year yields also pushed toward 5% around **19 October 2023**, strengthening the dollar broadly. Turkey, a large net energy importer, is sensitive to both channels.

### Bottom Line

The break marks a modest, orderly step-up in USD/TRY consistent with the managed depreciation regime in place since mid-2023 — a continuation and slight acceleration of trend, not a discrete shock. Given the low detection confidence and the absence of any change in trend direction or increase in volatility, this break should be treated as **low-significance** and not used as evidence of a policy or market regime shift.
