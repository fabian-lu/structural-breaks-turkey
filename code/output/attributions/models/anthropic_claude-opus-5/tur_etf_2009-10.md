## Structural Break Analysis: TUR, October 2009

### What changed
The level of the series shifted up sharply: mean USD price rose from **25.33 to 38.23 (+51%, ~+4.9 in normalized units)**. Equally notable, dispersion **fell from 7.50 to 5.43** (coefficient of variation from ~30% to ~14%). This is the signature of a *regime change*, not a spike: the ETF moved from a wide, crisis-era trading range into a higher, calmer post-crisis range.

Given monthly data, the comparison windows effectively span the crisis trough (late 2008–mid 2009) versus the recovery (late 2009–2010). The "decreasing trend" flags on both sides are local window artifacts — the crisis window ends in a rebound, and the post-break window includes the mid-2010 EM wobble — and should not be read as a persistent downtrend.

### Likely drivers
1. **Global post-crisis risk-on rally.** Global equities bottomed in March 2009; EM equity/bond inflows accelerated through Q3–Q4 2009 as the dollar weakened and G4 policy rates went to zero. Turkey, a high-beta EM, was among the biggest beneficiaries — the ISE-100 roughly doubled off its November 2008 low during 2009.
2. **Lira appreciation (double-counted in USD terms).** USD/TRY recovered from roughly 1.80 in March 2009 toward ~1.45–1.50 by late 2009. Because TUR is unhedged, local equity gains and FX gains compounded.
3. **Aggressive CBRT easing.** The policy rate was cut from 16.75% (Nov 2008) to ~6.75% by late 2009 — over 1,000bp — a powerful re-rating impulse for Turkish equities as inflation collapsed and the economy exited its ~-14% y/y Q1-2009 contraction.
4. **Improved sovereign risk perception.** Turkish CDS narrowed materially through 2009; rating agencies moved to positive outlooks/upgrades over 2009–early 2010 (Fitch to BB+ in December 2009). *Speculative as a precise trigger for the October date.*
5. **Also worth noting:** on-again/off-again IMF standby program talks through 2009 (ultimately abandoned in March 2010) were a live news flow item, and Turkey hosted the **IMF/World Bank Annual Meetings in Istanbul on 6–7 October 2009**, coinciding with the break window — likely coincidental attention rather than a fundamental cause.

### Significance
The magnitude is large and economically meaningful (a >50% level shift with lower volatility), and the direction is consistent with well-documented macro developments. However, **confidence of 64.4% is only moderate**: the underlying process was trending strongly through 2009, so a smooth recovery is being approximated by a discrete step. The October 2009 date should be treated as an *approximate midpoint of a multi-month regime transition* (roughly March–December 2009), not a discrete event date. The volatility compression is the more robust piece of evidence that a genuine regime change — from crisis to recovery — occurred.
