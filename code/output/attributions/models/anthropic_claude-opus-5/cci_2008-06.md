## Structural Break Analysis: Turkey Consumer Confidence, June 2008

### What changed
The series shifted from a mildly optimistic regime to a pessimistic one. The mean fell from **101.92 to 97.53** (a ~4.4-point decline in window means; estimated level shift of **2.12 points** after controlling for trend), moving the index decisively below the 100 long-run-average threshold. Dispersion was essentially unchanged (σ 2.21 → 2.03), so this is a **level shift, not a volatility regime change**.

Equally notable is the **change in trend direction**: the pre-break period was already deteriorating, while the post-break window trends upward. This pattern — a step down onto a lower plateau followed by gradual recovery — is characteristic of a shock absorption and rebound cycle rather than a permanent regime change.

### Likely drivers
The timing coincides with several overlapping shocks; attribution is plausible but not identified by the data alone:

- **Commodity/inflation shock (H1 2008):** Brent crude rose toward its July 2008 peak (~$147/bbl) alongside a global food price spike. Turkish CPI inflation accelerated to roughly 10–11% by mid-2008, sharply eroding real household purchasing power — typically the dominant driver of Turkish consumer sentiment.
- **Monetary tightening:** The CBRT reversed course and raised the policy rate in May–July 2008 (from ~15.25% toward 16.75%), tightening credit conditions for households.
- **Domestic political uncertainty:** The AKP closure case before the Constitutional Court (decided 30 July 2008) and the Ergenekon indictments (July 2008) created a period of elevated political risk premia and lira/equity volatility.
- **Global financial stress:** Mid-2008 marked the escalation phase of the global crisis (Bear Stearns in March, Fannie/Freddie and Lehman in September), which hit Turkey's export demand and capital inflows. Note the June break *precedes* the acute crisis phase — the deeper collapse in sentiment came in late 2008/early 2009, with GDP contracting ~4.8% in 2009 and unemployment peaking near 16%.

### Significance assessment
**Moderate.** Statistical confidence is 67% — suggestive but below conventional thresholds. The shift magnitude (2.12) is roughly one within-regime standard deviation, so the break is modest relative to normal month-to-month noise. Part of the apparent discontinuity may reflect the pre-existing downward trend rather than a discrete jump, and the amplitude-adjustment procedure compresses cyclical swings, dampening what was in raw terms a much sharper deterioration.

**Interpretation:** best read as the statistically detectable **onset** of the 2008–09 crisis regime in Turkish consumer sentiment, rather than a sharp, isolated event. The upward post-break trend is consistent with the 2009–10 recovery, implying a temporary cyclical downshift rather than a structural reset of the confidence level.
