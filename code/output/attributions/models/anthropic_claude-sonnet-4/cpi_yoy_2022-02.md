## Structural Break Analysis: Turkish Inflation (February 2022)

### What Changed
A highly significant structural break occurred in Turkish consumer price inflation in February 2022, representing a dramatic upward shift of **38.42 percentage points**. The inflation rate jumped from an average of 16.14% in the pre-break period to 64.51% in the post-break period - a **300% increase** in the inflation level.

### Statistical Significance
- **Confidence Level**: 95% - indicating very strong statistical evidence
- **Volatility**: Increased from 7.92% to 12.56% standard deviation, showing greater price instability
- **Trend Reversal**: The pre-break increasing trend shifted to a decreasing trend post-break, suggesting the peak inflationary pressure occurred around the break point

### Likely Causes
The timing strongly suggests this break was triggered by **Russia's invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022**. Key contributing factors likely include:

1. **Energy Price Shock**: Turkey's heavy reliance on Russian energy imports created immediate supply disruptions and price spikes
2. **Food Security Crisis**: Ukraine and Russia are major grain exporters; the conflict severely impacted global food prices
3. **Currency Pressure**: Geopolitical instability and commodity price shocks likely intensified pressure on the Turkish lira
4. **Supply Chain Disruptions**: The conflict disrupted critical trade routes through the Black Sea region

### Assessment
This represents an **extreme inflationary shock** - among the most severe in Turkey's recent economic history. The magnitude suggests this was not a gradual policy-driven change but rather an acute external crisis response. The subsequent decreasing trend indicates potential policy interventions or market adjustments attempting to contain the inflationary spiral.

The break's timing and magnitude make it a textbook example of how geopolitical events can create immediate, severe macroeconomic disruptions in vulnerable economies.
