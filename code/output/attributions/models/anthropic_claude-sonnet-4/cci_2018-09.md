## Structural Break Analysis: Turkey Consumer Confidence (September 2018)

### What Changed
The Turkish consumer confidence index experienced a **downward structural shift of 2.93 points** in September 2018, dropping from an average of 101.12 (slightly above long-run optimism) to 97.89 (below-average pessimism). This represents a transition from above-average to below-average consumer sentiment.

### Key Characteristics
- **Magnitude**: Moderate but persistent decline in confidence levels
- **Volatility**: Reduced after the break (std dev: 0.51 → 0.39), suggesting the new pessimistic state stabilized
- **Trend reversal**: Pre-break decreasing trend shifted to increasing, indicating potential recovery efforts

### Likely Causes
The timing strongly suggests this break relates to **Turkey's currency crisis of August-September 2018**:

- **Turkish Lira collapse**: The lira lost ~40% of its value against the USD in August 2018, peaking around August 13th
- **Inflation surge**: Consumer prices accelerated rapidly, eroding purchasing power
- **Economic uncertainty**: The crisis created widespread concern about Turkey's economic stability
- **Policy responses**: Emergency measures and interest rate hikes in September may have contributed to the trend reversal observed post-break

### Significance Assessment
This is a **moderately significant** structural change with 67.4% confidence. The break captures a clear shift from cautious optimism to sustained pessimism, reflecting how the currency crisis fundamentally altered Turkish consumers' economic outlook. The subsequent stabilization at lower levels suggests this represented a genuine regime change rather than temporary volatility.
