Based on the time series data provided, here is an analysis of the structural break in Turkish consumer price inflation (YoY):

### 1. Description of the Change
* **Direction and Magnitude:** There is a massive upward shift in Turkish annual consumer price inflation of **38.42 percentage points**. 
* **Level Shift:** In the 30-day window prior to February 1, 2022, the mean inflation rate was **16.14%** (with an increasing trend from a relatively lower base). Immediately following the break, the mean inflation rate surged to **64.51%**.
* **Volatility:** The standard deviation increased from 7.92 to 12.56, indicating that inflation became not only much higher but also significantly more volatile in the post-break period.

### 2. Possible Causes and Timing Context
* **Monetary Policy Context:** The timing directly follows an unorthodox monetary policy easing cycle implemented by the Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (CBRT) in late 2021. Despite rising global and domestic price pressures, the CBRT cut its policy rate by 500 basis points between September and December 2021 (lowering the one-week repo rate from 19% to 14%). 
* **Exchange Rate Pass-Through:** These rate cuts triggered a severe currency crisis in late 2021, leading to a sharp depreciation of the Turkish Lira (TRY) against major currencies. Given Turkey's heavy reliance on imported energy and raw materials, this currency crash generated an immediate and massive pass-through effect into domestic consumer prices by early 2022.
* **External Shocks (*Speculative/Contextual*):** While domestic monetary policy was the primary driver, this period also coincided with the onset of the Russia-Ukraine conflict in late February 2022, which exacerbated global energy and commodity price shocks. *(Note: While the war began slightly after the exact break date of Feb 1, escalating geopolitical tensions in the preceding weeks contributed to the broader inflationary environment).*

### 3. Significance Assessment
* **Statistical Significance:** With a 95.0% confidence level and a nearly 40-percentage-point jump in the mean, this structural break is **highly significant**. It marks a definitive regime shift from moderate-to-high inflation to chronic, hyper-inflationary dynamics characterized by rates exceeding 60%. 

### 4. Summary
The structural break on February 1, 2022, represents a critical macroeconomic turning point for Turkey. It captures the immediate inflationary fallout of aggressive interest rate cuts and the ensuing currency depreciation of late 2021, fundamentally altering the country's inflation trajectory.
