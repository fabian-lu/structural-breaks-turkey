### 1. Description of Change
On **June 1, 2008**, a structural break occurred in the OECD Consumer Confidence Index for Turkey, marked by a downward shift of **2.12 standard deviations**. 

* **Before the break (May 2008):** The index averaged 101.92, reflecting above-average consumer optimism (the long-run average is ~100), though with a mildly decreasing trend.
* **After the break (June/July 2008):** The mean dropped sharply to 97.53, crossing the threshold from optimism into pessimism (<100). Despite the immediate drop, the short-term post-break window exhibits a mildly increasing trend (mean 97.53, std dev 2.03), indicating a stabilization at a lower baseline rather than a freefall.
* **Significance:** The magnitude (2.12) and the crossing of the neutral 100-point threshold indicate a statistically significant and economically meaningful regime shift toward consumer pessimism.

### 2. Possible Causes and External Events
The timing of this break—mid-2008—coincides with the severe escalation of the **Global Financial Crisis (GFC)**, triggered by the U.S. subprime mortgage collapse. 

* **Macroeconomic Factors:** Turkey’s emerging market economy was highly vulnerable to global liquidity contractions, rising risk aversion, and surging global commodity prices (oil and food inflation peaked in mid-2008). 
* **External Context (Speculative regarding exact domestic transmission):** While the collapse of Lehman Brothers occurred later in September 2008, global financial markets and investor sentiment deteriorated rapidly in the second quarter of 2008. Turkish consumers likely anticipated imported inflation, credit tightening, and a slowdown in domestic economic activity. 

### 3. Summary
The June 2008 structural break represents a sudden downward adjustment in Turkish consumer sentiment, shifting the populace from optimism to pessimism. This was driven primarily by external macroeconomic shocks associated with the onset of the Global Financial Crisis, which impacted emerging market confidence through inflation pressures and tightening financial conditions.
