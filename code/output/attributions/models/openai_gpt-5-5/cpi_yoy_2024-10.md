In October 2024, Turkish year-on-year consumer price inflation shows a **clear downward level shift**. The estimated break is a **17.79 percentage-point downshift**, with the local mean falling from about **64.0% before the break** to **37.5% after**. Inflation was already on a **declining trend** before the break and continued declining afterward, so this looks less like a sudden reversal and more like an **acceleration/step-down in an existing disinflation process**. Volatility also fell, with the standard deviation declining from **12.9** to **5.5**, suggesting a more stable post-break path.

Potential reasons include:

- **Base effects:** Turkish inflation had been very high in late 2023 and early 2024, so annual inflation rates in late 2024 mechanically declined as earlier large monthly price increases dropped out of the year-on-year comparison.
- **Tight monetary policy:** The Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye had raised the policy rate sharply, reaching **50% in March 2024**, and maintained a tight stance through this period. This likely contributed to weaker demand and slower price growth, though with lags.
- **Policy credibility and lira stabilization:** If exchange-rate pressures eased around this period, that would reduce pass-through into import and consumer prices. This is plausible but should be treated as supportive rather than definitive evidence from the inflation series alone.

The statistical confidence is **68.6%**, which is **moderate rather than strong**. The magnitude is economically meaningful, but because the pre-break trend was already decreasing, the break should be interpreted as a **significant downward shift within an ongoing disinflation trend**, not as a completely new inflation regime on its own.
