In October 2009, TUR appears to have experienced an **upward level shift** in USD terms. The average price rose from about **$25.33 before the break to $38.23 after**, an increase of roughly **$12.90, or 51%**. The detected break magnitude is **+4.92**, indicating a positive shift, although both the pre- and post-break short-window trends were still **decreasing**, so this looks more like a **higher price regime** than the start of a sustained uptrend.

The most likely explanation is the **post-global-financial-crisis rebound in emerging-market risk assets**. By late 2009, global liquidity conditions had improved, investors were moving back into higher-risk EM equities, and Turkish equities benefited from the broader recovery. Because TUR is priced in **US dollars**, the move also likely reflects a combination of **local Turkish equity gains and stabilization or appreciation pressure in the Turkish lira** after the crisis period.

Potentially relevant context includes:
- The global equity recovery following the March 2009 lows.
- Improved EM investor sentiment through mid-to-late 2009.
- Turkey-specific recovery expectations after the sharp 2008–2009 downturn.
- Later supportive sovereign-rating actions, such as Fitch’s Turkey upgrade in **December 2009**, though this occurred after the detected break and should be viewed as confirming improving sentiment rather than necessarily causing the October shift.

The statistical confidence is **64.4%**, which is **moderate rather than strong**. The size of the mean shift is economically meaningful, but the evidence does not point to a single clear event on 2009-10-01. Overall, the break most likely reflects a **regime repricing of Turkish equities in USD during the global post-crisis recovery**, rather than a discrete Turkey-specific shock.
