In June 2008, Turkey’s OECD consumer confidence index appears to have shifted down from an above-average level to a below-average level.

- **What changed:** The local mean fell from **101.9** before the break to **97.5** after it, a decline of about **4.4 index points**. This moved sentiment from slightly optimistic to clearly pessimistic relative to the long-run average of 100.
- **Direction and persistence:** The pre-break trend was already **decreasing**, suggesting weakening confidence before June. After the break, the trend turned **increasing**, but from a lower base, implying a downward level shift rather than a continued collapse.
- **Statistical significance:** With a reported break magnitude of **2.12** and relatively low local volatility of about **2 points**, the change is meaningful. However, the stated **67% confidence** suggests the evidence is moderate rather than definitive.
- **Possible reasons:** The timing coincides with rising global financial stress before the 2008 crisis peak, high food and energy prices, and worsening inflation pressures. In Turkey specifically, political uncertainty around the **AKP closure case filed in March 2008**, with a court decision later on **30 July 2008**, may also have weighed on households. These are plausible contributors, but the data alone cannot identify a single cause.

Overall, the break reflects a moderate but economically relevant deterioration in Turkish consumer sentiment around mid-2008, likely linked to a combination of global financial concerns, inflation pressures, and domestic political uncertainty.
