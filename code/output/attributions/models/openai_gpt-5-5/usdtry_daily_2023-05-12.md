On **2023-05-12**, USD/TRY shows an **upward structural break**, meaning the Turkish lira weakened against the U.S. dollar.

**What changed**
- The average exchange rate rose from **19.37** before the break to **21.45** after it — an increase of about **2.08 TRY per USD**, or roughly **10.7%**.
- Volatility increased sharply: the 30-day standard deviation rose from **0.11** to **1.69**, suggesting the exchange rate moved from a tightly managed/stable regime into a much more volatile depreciation phase.
- The trend was increasing both before and after, but the post-break period shows a much faster and less stable rise.

**Potential reasons**
- The break occurred just before Turkey’s **presidential and parliamentary elections on 2023-05-14**, with a runoff on **2023-05-28**. Election-related uncertainty likely contributed to pressure on the lira.
- Around this period, markets were focused on Turkey’s unorthodox monetary policy, high inflation, pressure on FX reserves, and expectations that exchange-rate management could become less sustainable after the election.
- After the election, policy changes and reduced intervention expectations may have contributed to a faster depreciation. For example, Mehmet Şimşek was appointed finance minister on **2023-06-03**, which markets interpreted as a possible shift toward more orthodox policy, but also toward allowing the lira to adjust.

**Significance assessment**
- The reported break confidence is **53.8%**, so statistically this is only a **moderate/weakly confirmed break** at the exact date.
- However, the economic change is meaningful: the post-break mean is much higher and volatility increased dramatically. This suggests a real regime shift in USD/TRY behavior, even if the precise break date should be interpreted cautiously.
