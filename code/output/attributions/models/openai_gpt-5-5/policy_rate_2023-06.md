Around **2023-06-01**, Turkey’s overnight interbank/call-money rate shows a clear **upward structural break**, indicating a sharp tightening in short-term monetary conditions.

- **What changed:** The rate shifted upward substantially. The detected level shift is **+12.28 percentage points**, while the 30-day mean rose from about **13.1% before the break** to **40.1% after**. The pre-break pattern was **decreasing**, but after the break the rate became **strongly increasing**, with volatility also rising notably.
- **Statistical significance:** With **92.2% confidence**, this is a meaningful break, though not absolute certainty. The size of the shift and the reversal in trend make it economically significant.
- **Likely reasons:** The timing aligns closely with Turkey’s post-election policy pivot in mid-2023. After the May 2023 elections, the authorities moved toward more orthodox monetary policy. Key events include:
  - **3 June 2023:** Mehmet Şimşek was appointed Treasury and Finance Minister, signaling a shift toward tighter, more conventional policy.
  - **9 June 2023:** Hafize Gaye Erkan became CBRT governor.
  - **22 June 2023:** CBRT raised the one-week repo rate from **8.5% to 15%**, starting a tightening cycle that continued through 2023.
- **Interpretation:** The break likely reflects markets and the banking system repricing short-term funding conditions in anticipation of, and then response to, the CBRT’s tightening cycle. The increase in volatility suggests adjustment frictions and uncertainty during the transition away from the prior low-rate policy framework.
