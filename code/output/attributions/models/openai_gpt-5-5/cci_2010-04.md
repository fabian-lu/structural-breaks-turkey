In April 2010, Turkey’s OECD consumer confidence index appears to have shifted upward materially.

- **What changed:** The mean level rose from **97.43 before the break to 101.15 after it**, an increase of about **3.7 index points**. This moved confidence from **below the long-run average**—indicating pessimism—to **slightly above average**, indicating renewed optimism. The short-term pattern also changed from **decreasing before the break** to **increasing afterward**, while volatility fell from **2.31 to 0.81**, suggesting a more stable improvement.

- **Statistical significance:** The reported break magnitude is **2.33**, with **69.2% confidence**. That suggests a **moderate but not overwhelming** structural break: the shift is economically meaningful, especially because it crosses the 100 threshold, but the statistical evidence is not extremely strong.

- **Potential reasons:** The timing aligns with Turkey’s recovery from the **2008–2009 global financial crisis**. By early 2010, Turkey was experiencing a rebound in activity, supported by improving domestic demand, credit conditions, and global trade. Turkey’s GDP growth turned strongly positive in 2010 after the 2009 contraction, and improving labor-market and production indicators likely contributed to better household sentiment. These macro-recovery factors are plausible explanations, though the data alone does not identify a specific causal event.

Overall, the break suggests a transition in April 2010 from weak, deteriorating consumer sentiment to a more optimistic and stable confidence regime, likely linked to the broader post-crisis economic recovery.
