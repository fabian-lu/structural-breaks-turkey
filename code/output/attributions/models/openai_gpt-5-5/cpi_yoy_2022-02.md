The February 2022 break marks a large upward level shift in Turkish year-on-year consumer price inflation.

- **What changed:** Inflation moved from a previously rising but much lower regime, with a pre-break mean of about **16.1%**, to a much higher post-break regime averaging about **64.5%**. The estimated structural shift is **+38.4 percentage points**, indicating a persistent jump in inflation rather than normal month-to-month volatility.
- **Statistical significance:** The break is detected at **95% confidence**, and the size of the shift is large relative to the pre-break standard deviation, so it appears economically and statistically meaningful.
- **Likely drivers:** The timing is consistent with Turkey’s late-2021 currency and inflation shock. The Turkish lira depreciated sharply in late 2021 after a series of policy rate cuts by the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye from **September to December 2021**, despite already elevated inflation. This likely fed rapidly into import prices, energy costs, and broader consumer prices by early 2022.
- **Additional pressures:** Large administered price increases in **January 2022**, including electricity and natural gas tariffs, likely contributed. The Russia–Ukraine war began on **24 February 2022**, just after the break date, and may have intensified food and energy inflation afterward, though it was not the initial cause of the February break.
- **Interpretation:** The data suggest a transition from moderately high and rising inflation to an extreme high-inflation regime. The subsequent “decreasing” trend after the break should be interpreted cautiously, since the post-break mean remains very elevated.
