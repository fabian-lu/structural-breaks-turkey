Around **2011-08-01**, TUR shows a detected **downward level shift of about $10.45**, but the statistical evidence is weak.

- **What changed:** The model flags a negative break, implying TUR fell materially relative to its prior trajectory. However, the simple window averages do **not** show a lower post-break level: the mean rose from **$35.80 before** to **$39.03 after**, and the trend was **increasing both before and after**. Volatility also declined, from **8.95** to **6.20**. This suggests the break may reflect a short, sharp drawdown or disruption rather than a clean persistent regime shift.
- **Potential reasons:** Early August 2011 coincided with a major global risk-off episode: the **U.S. debt-ceiling crisis and S&P downgrade of U.S. sovereign debt on 2011-08-05**, plus intensifying **Eurozone sovereign-debt stress**. Emerging-market assets sold off broadly, and Turkey was especially sensitive because TUR is priced in USD and reflects both Turkish equity performance and the Turkish lira. Any lira weakness would have amplified USD-denominated losses.
- **Turkey-specific context:** Turkey also faced concerns in 2011 around a large current-account deficit and currency vulnerability. The Turkish central bank cut its policy rate on **2011-08-04**, which may have contributed to lira pressure, though attributing the ETF move directly to this is speculative.
- **Significance:** The reported confidence is only **40.8%**, and the post-break mean is actually higher than the pre-break mean. Therefore, this should be treated as a **low-confidence, possibly transient market-shock signal**, not strong evidence of a durable downward structural break.
