### **Structural Break Analysis: iShares MSCI Turkey ETF (TUR) – August 2011**

#### **1. What Changed?**
- **Magnitude & Direction:** A **10.45-point downward shift** in the ETF’s price occurred on **August 1, 2011**, marking a significant break in the trend.
- **Mean & Volatility:**
  - **Before Break (July 2011):** Mean = **$35.80**, Std Dev = **8.95**, Trend = **increasing**
  - **After Break (August 2011):** Mean = **$39.03**, Std Dev = **6.20**, Trend = **increasing**
- **Interpretation:** Despite the downward break, the **post-break mean is higher** than the pre-break mean, suggesting that while the level shifted downward, the **trend remained upward** but with **lower volatility**.

#### **2. Possible Causes (Based on Timing & Data)**
- **Turkish Political & Economic Shifts (2011):**
  - **June 2011 General Elections:** The ruling AKP won a third term, but political tensions and protests were rising.
  - **Syrian Civil War Escalation (Mid-2011):** Turkey’s involvement in regional conflicts may have increased uncertainty.
  - **Global Risk Aversion (August 2011):** The **U.S. debt ceiling crisis** (Aug 2, 2011) and **European sovereign debt concerns** led to a global sell-off in emerging markets.

- **Turkish Lira Depreciation (2011):**
  - The **TRY lost ~15% against USD in 2011**, reducing USD-denominated returns for foreign investors.

#### **3. External Events Near Break Date**
- **August 2, 2011:** U.S. debt ceiling crisis (S&P downgrades U.S. credit rating).
- **July-August 2011:** Escalation of Syrian conflict, increasing regional instability.
- **June 12, 2011:** Turkish elections (AKP retains power but faces growing opposition).

#### **4. Significance of the Break**
- **Confidence Level (40.8%):** Relatively low, suggesting the break may not be statistically strong.
- **Volatility Decrease:** The **reduction in standard deviation (8.95 → 6.20)** indicates a stabilization post-break.
- **Trend Persistence:** Despite the break, the **upward trend continued**, meaning the break was more of a **level shift** rather than a trend reversal.

#### **5. Conclusion**
The **August 2011 break** likely reflects **short-term panic due to global risk-off sentiment** (U.S. debt crisis, European turmoil) rather than a fundamental shift in Turkish equities. The **TRY depreciation** and **regional instability** may have contributed, but the **low confidence level** suggests caution in overinterpreting the break.
