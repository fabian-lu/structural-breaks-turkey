### **Structural Break Analysis: OECD Consumer Confidence Index (Turkey) – 2018-09-01**

#### **1. Description of the Change**
- **Magnitude & Direction:** The consumer confidence index experienced a **sharp downward shift of 2.93 points** (from ~101.12 to ~97.89), moving from **above-average optimism (101.12) to below-average optimism (97.89)**.
- **Trend Shift:** Before the break, confidence was **decreasing** (likely in a mild downturn), while after the break, it began **increasing** (suggesting a partial recovery from the new lower baseline).

#### **2. Potential Causes (Timing & Statistical Evidence)**
- **Pre-Break Decline (Before Sept 2018):** The downward trend suggests **gradual erosion of consumer sentiment**, possibly due to:
  - **Inflation pressures** (Turkey’s inflation peaked at **~25% in Oct 2018**, eroding purchasing power).
  - **Currency depreciation** (TRY lost ~30% against USD in 2018, increasing import costs).
  - **Monetary tightening** (CBRT raised rates aggressively in 2018, increasing borrowing costs).
- **Break Point (Sept 2018):** The sudden drop aligns with **heightened economic instability**, particularly:
  - **Aug 2018 U.S. sanctions** (following Turkey’s detention of a U.S. pastor, worsening financial conditions).
  - **IMF speculation** (markets feared a potential IMF bailout, increasing uncertainty).
  - **Corporate debt concerns** (Turkish firms with high FX debt faced refinancing risks).

#### **3. External Events Near the Break Date**
- **Aug 10, 2018:** U.S. doubles steel/aluminum tariffs on Turkey, worsening trade tensions.
- **Aug 13, 2018:** Turkey’s central bank hikes rates sharply (from 17.75% to 24%) to defend the lira.
- **Sept 2018:** IMF discussions gain traction (though no formal program was announced).
- **Oct 2018:** Inflation hits **25.2% (YoY)**, the highest in 15 years.

#### **4. Significance of the Change**
- **Statistical Significance:** The **2.93-point drop is large relative to pre-break volatility (0.51 std dev)**, indicating a **structurally significant shift** in consumer sentiment.
- **Economic Impact:** The decline pushed confidence **below the long-run average (100)**, signaling **worsening household expectations** about future economic conditions.
- **Persistence:** The post-break **increasing trend** suggests a **partial rebound**, but confidence remained **below pre-crisis levels**, indicating lingering pessimism.

#### **5. Conclusion**
The structural break in **Sept 2018** reflects a **sharp deterioration in consumer confidence**, driven by **currency crisis, inflation spikes, and policy uncertainty**. While confidence later stabilized, it did not fully recover to pre-crisis optimism, highlighting **long-lasting damage to household sentiment**.

*(Note: While external sanctions and inflation were key drivers, the exact causal attribution requires further econometric testing.)*
